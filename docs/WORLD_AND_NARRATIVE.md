# World and Narrative

All names and story details are working canon until a narrative lock. This document establishes the original creative grammar and vertical-slice material.

## Premise

Long ago, the world did not break; it was **folded** to survive a sky-wide disaster. Valleys, coastlines, roads, and even memories now lie across adjacent facets of the same place. Most people experience only the facet made stable by their town's bell, beacon, mill, or ritual. A few **Turnbearers** can hear the tension between facets and safely reframe a local fold.

The old network of stabilizing bells is falling out of tune. The failures first appear as ordinary problems—missing paths, rooms no one remembers, crops shadowed at noon—before revealing a deliberate attempt to force the world into one “perfect” unchanging face.

The story asks: **Is a safe world still alive if it permits only one way to be seen?**

## Rules of folding

Narrative and mechanics share the same rules:

1. A facet is a neighboring arrangement of the same physical place, not another infinite universe.
2. Matter cannot be freely duplicated by turning. Living beings keep continuity and cannot meet alternate copies of themselves.
3. Stable anchors allow safe quarter-turns; uncontrolled folds are dangerous, disorienting, and locally destructive.
4. People remember committed turns, but long-isolated communities may preserve conflicting histories of how a place “always” looked.
5. Turning reveals or aligns latent structure; it cannot solve arbitrary emotional or political problems.
6. Every large change has a cost: tension moves elsewhere, an anchor wears, or a previously sheltered truth becomes visible.

These rules prevent the mechanic from becoming magic that excuses any plot development.

## Tone

- Wistful, curious, humane, and lightly funny.
- Ordinary work and community life matter beside mythic stakes.
- Antagonists have comprehensible desires but are not absolved of harm.
- Mystery comes from incompatible perspectives and physical evidence, not endless proper nouns.
- Hope is practiced through repair, compromise, and attention.

Violence is stylized and low-gore. Horror may come from impossible space, erasure, or isolation, not graphic suffering.

## Vertical-slice region

### Brindlewick

A hillside market town built around the **Tidemark Bell**, a civic chime that keeps its streets aligned between the orchard slope and old river road. Its roofs overlap like scales around a blue-gray bell tower. The town's primary loop connects the south gate, market, bell square, inn terrace, and workshop lane.

Current problem: the bell rings a note no resident admits hearing. A familiar footpath intermittently ends at a blank wall, and the apprentice responsible for routine tuning has disappeared from the day's public story.

Design functions:

- Teaches compact-town navigation and resident state changes.
- Introduces Peek through layered roofs and foreground laundry.
- Uses the first Reveal turn to expose a service alley behind the bell works.
- Establishes that a changed perspective can reveal social omission, not only treasure.

### Thimblewood Verge

A short field route of hedges, old survey stones, and broad fungal shelves between town and grotto. The path teaches visible encounters and shows that wildlife reacts to unstable facets.

Design functions:

- Two ordinary encounters with avoidable representatives.
- An optional resident vignette and recovery nook.
- One route shortcut opened from the far side.
- A safe Align turn that joins two halves of an old survey stair.

### Bellroot Grotto

A mineral cavern wrapped around the buried root of an older anchor. Copper guides carry the town bell's resonance underground. Someone has retuned them into a rigid single pattern, stranding the missing apprentice between routes.

Design functions:

- Combines Reveal and Align with battle intent.
- Provides the first unmistakable evidence of deliberate sabotage.
- Ends with a boss built around interrupting a dangerous startup rather than racing damage.
- Returns the party to a visibly changed Brindlewick at dusk.

## Initial cast

### Mara Venn — provisional protagonist

A route surveyor who draws practical maps and distrusts mystical explanations until they become measurable. Mara can hear fold tension as a rhythm that arrives slightly before nearby sounds. Her personal trait, **Measure Twice**, improves information gained from inspecting enemies or uncertain routes.

Arc seed: she begins by believing every disagreement can be solved with a more accurate map, then confronts truths that are relational rather than geometric.

### Ori Pell — bellwright apprentice

Bright, stubborn, and socially invisible whenever senior craft workers tell the town's story. Ori discovered the sabotage, attempted a repair alone, and became stranded in Bellroot. Ori's personal trait, **Carry the Note**, preserves part of a helpful temporary effect when its source ends.

Arc seed: expertise without authority, and the danger of proving oneself through isolation.

### Sen Ilex — itinerant Cantor

A performer who recognizes old anchor songs and initially presents their knowledge as folklore to avoid official attention. Sen uses humor to test whether a group is safe. Their personal trait, **Pickup Beat**, grants a small Focus benefit after following another party member's action family.

Arc seed: moving from detached witness to accountable participant.

### Warden Hale Rusk — local authority

Brindlewick's practical, exhausted warden. Rusk has hidden the anomalous ringing to prevent a market panic and sincerely believes containment is responsible leadership. The concealment makes the rescue harder.

Rusk is neither a secret mastermind nor a disposable obstacle; the slice lets the player challenge the decision and see the immediate civic consequence.

## Antagonistic force

The **Stillwork** is a loose program of engineers, officials, and survivors who believe instability can end only when each place is fixed into a single authorized facet. Their methods erase routes, local histories, and eventually people who cannot be reconciled with the chosen arrangement.

The vertical slice does not name or explain the full organization. It shows a precise retuning tool and a repeated symbol with a plausible practical use. Mystery should create a question the player can phrase, not merely an unexplained proper noun.

## Slice narrative flow

1. Mara reaches Brindlewick for a routine survey and hears the impossible second note.
2. A short market walk establishes the bell, town loop, Rusk, Sen, and the missing-but-unmentioned Ori.
3. Peek reveals an unattended tool through a layered workshop window.
4. The first World Turn exposes the sealed service alley and evidence of Ori's route.
5. Rusk admits the concealment; the player chooses a firm or empathetic response that changes later dialogue, not quest access.
6. Sen joins for the Thimblewood route and teaches the first Calling interaction through play.
7. The party reaches Bellroot, finds Ori, and learns the anchor was deliberately retuned.
8. The boss weaponizes a fold startup; the player reads intent, breaks Poise, and realigns the path.
9. Back in Brindlewick, Ori is publicly credited or privately protected based on a player conversation. The bell now rings a stable harmony rather than its former single note.
10. A final reframed town view reveals the Stillwork mark on a distant survey post, pointing beyond the slice.

## Dialogue voice

- Prefer concrete observation and work vocabulary over lore exposition.
- Let residents disagree in diction, priorities, and what they notice.
- Keep ordinary lines short enough to read while remembering the scene.
- Use subtext, action, and environment before ellipses or vague mystery speech.
- Jokes arise from character and circumstance, not references to other games or modern meme phrasing.
- Do not give every resident encyclopedic knowledge of folds.
- Repeat a key term only when repetition teaches or emotionally transforms it.

Example tone—not production dialogue:

> The bell is never late. People are late. Weather is late. The bell has standards.

## Choice design

Choices in the slice express the player's Mara rather than branch into separate levels. They affect:

- whether Rusk trusts the party's discretion
- whether Ori receives public credit immediately
- which resident offers the optional return vignette
- several later lines and one modest consumable reward

The journal states consequences in-world and never labels a compassionate response as the mechanically “good” choice.

## Naming language

- Settlements combine tactile craft, landscape, plant, weather, or old-route words: Brindlewick, Bellroot, Thimblewood.
- Personal names are short, pronounceable, and varied without using punctuation as exoticism.
- Callings use occupational or musical nouns that communicate play.
- Technical fold vocabulary is small: facet, anchor, turn, tension, alignment, stilling.

Maintain a pronunciation field for voiced efforts such as exertions or trailer narration even though full voice acting is out of scope.

## Narrative content boundaries

- No chosen-one bloodline. Turnbearing is a learned sensitivity with unequal access, not biological supremacy.
- No culture exists solely to provide ruins or wisdom to the protagonist.
- No companion is only a combat kit or exposition device.
- No false choice: if outcomes converge, the emotional and relational response must still be visibly different.
- No missable lore required to understand the main conflict.
- No reference-game characters, plots, terminology, map shapes, or signature sequences.

## Continuity checklist

- Does the event obey all six folding rules?
- Does a World Turn solve a spatial problem rather than an arbitrary human conflict?
- Who wanted what before the party arrived?
- What visibly changes in the space or residents afterward?
- What does each present companion notice that another would miss?
- Can the player state the immediate goal and the unanswered question?
- Is required context available through more than one fragile optional line?
- Are condition flags, journal text, and scene staging consistent for every reachable branch?
