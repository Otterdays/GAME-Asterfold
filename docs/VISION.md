# Product Vision

## One-sentence pitch

**Asterfold is a character-led party RPG in which players explore warm, hand-built sprite dioramas, turn the viewpoint to reveal the world's hidden folds, and shape a four-person party through mix-and-match Callings.**

## Player promise

The player enters a world that feels small enough to know and strange enough to keep unfolding. Walking through a town should feel like handling a beautiful miniature: roofs pass in the foreground, windows glow through depth, people turn as the camera moves, and a deliberate quarter-turn can expose an alley, bridge, secret, or entirely different reading of the same place.

The mechanical promise is equally direct: time spent experimenting with the party produces new strategies, not merely larger numbers. Battles communicate what will happen and when. Towns reward attention to people and place. The game lets the player stop, save, read, remap, and reduce motion without punishment.

## Experience pillars

### A world worth turning

- 2D character animation supplies warmth and expression.
- Real 3D composition supplies parallax, elevation, lighting, foreground occlusion, and physical presence.
- A gentle **Peek Orbit** makes spaces feel tangible without changing rules.
- Authored **World Turns** commit a new facet and may reveal traversable routes.
- Every turn must reveal useful information, emotion, navigation, or play; it is never camera spectacle alone.

### Small places with deep memory

- Settlements are compact enough to form a mental map after one visit.
- Strong landmarks, circular routes, shortcuts, and sightlines eliminate dead walking.
- NPC placement and dialogue change after meaningful events.
- Revisits offer consequence and texture, not repeated chores.

### A party that becomes yours

- Each character has personal identity without a permanent combat role.
- **Callings** provide action sets and stat tendencies.
- Mastery unlocks a limited cross-Calling technique or passive, producing legible combinations.
- Encounters are authored to reward several solutions rather than one required build.

### Momentum with kindness

- No random battles; encounters are visible and usually avoidable.
- Common transitions are short and skippable after first viewing.
- Dialogue, movement, and battle pacing can be accelerated.
- Saving is generous and failure preserves useful progress.
- Accessibility settings are designed with the mechanic, not added after it.

## Audience and positioning

Primary audience:

- Players who enjoy classic party RPG structure but want modern pacing and usability.
- Players drawn to pixel art who still want depth, lighting, and camera presence.
- Build-crafters who prefer readable interactions over opaque optimization.
- Explorers who enjoy authored secrets and compact spatial puzzles.

The tone sits between wistful adventure and lived-in warmth. It may become tense, eerie, or sad, but it is not cynical, nihilistic, or casually cruel.

## North-star play loop

1. Arrive in a visually distinctive compact place.
2. Read its landmarks, people, problem, and apparent routes.
3. Peek around the diorama and discover where a World Turn matters.
4. Use a turn, conversation, field skill, or party decision to open a path.
5. Face a short authored encounter that tests the current party idea.
6. Earn a new technique, relationship change, route, or piece of world truth.
7. Return through a place that now reads differently.

The loop should produce a meaningful payoff every 8–12 minutes and a comfortable stopping point every 20–30 minutes.

## Product shape

The production strategy is a polished 25–35 minute vertical slice first. A potential full game would target approximately 10–14 hours for the main path, with four active party members, six to eight deeply differentiated Callings, four major settlements, and a compact chapter structure. Those full-game numbers are planning bounds, not a commitment until the slice proves the experience.

Initial platforms are desktop Windows and Linux with controller and keyboard support. Architecture remains portable, but console certification, platform SDK work, and storefront commitments are outside the vertical slice.

## Measures of success

The vertical slice succeeds when representative players can:

- Understand movement, interaction, Peek Orbit, and a World Turn without a paragraph of tutorial text.
- Sketch the central town's main loop and landmark from memory after one visit.
- Explain why the world is 2D-in-3D as an experience, not simply name the rendering technique.
- Build two meaningfully different party strategies from the available Callings.
- Read enemy intent and predict action order before the final encounter.
- Complete the slice without mandatory grinding, motion discomfort, or an inaccessible control requirement.
- Recall at least one resident and one change caused by the party.

Technical success requires stable 60 fps on the reference machine, clean save/reload at every allowed state, no broken input focus, and no content validation errors.

## Creative boundaries

Asterfold can inherit principles from beloved RPGs: compact towns, job experimentation, expressive sprites, ensemble adventure, clear world rules, and melodic storytelling. It cannot inherit protected expression. All proper nouns, characters, maps, dialogue, art, UI composition, music, enemies, icons, and story sequences must be original.

## Explicit non-goals

- Photorealism or cinematic realism.
- A seamless open world.
- Hundreds of shallow abilities or loot tiers.
- Endless procedural content.
- Mandatory daily play, live-service systems, premium currencies, or social pressure.
- Real-time action combat requiring frame-perfect execution.
- Camera rotation as a constant dexterity challenge.
- A story whose stakes erase ordinary town life.
